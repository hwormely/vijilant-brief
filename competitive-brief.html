General AI documents.
Disaster-native AI acts.

Bonterra just shipped AI — note capture, activity summaries, early intervention signals. That's real, and it closes the documentation gap for general nonprofit work. But it doesn't know what FEMA IHP is. It doesn't understand the difference between a T2 survivor with lapsed ALE and one with a live insurance claim. It can't compute a Recovery Score from a 5-domain clinical assessment. RecoverPrepared and CaseWorthy have no AI at all. Vijilant's Auto-Triage Engine was built for the specific, structured context of disaster case management — and that is not something a general-purpose AI can replicate by adding a note-capture feature.

What we're
still shipping.

These aren't limitations to apologize for. They're the sequenced roadmap of a team that knows exactly what order to build things in.

Production deployment starts with LLP

Vijilant is in active development. The LLP pilot is the first production deployment — and every design and UX decision was made against real LLP caseload patterns, not theoretical personas. RecoverPrepared has more field time. That's real. We're building something built for more.

AI output is only as good as assessment input

The Auto-Triage Engine reads what the CM documents. Incomplete assessments produce lower-confidence triage outputs. This is surfaced in the UI and disclosed to CMs — it's not hidden. The solution is training and workflow enforcement, both of which are baked into the onboarding.

Multi-org configurability ships post-pilot

Every DCM organization will be able to import their own application templates and configure their intake questionnaire inside Vijilant without a services engagement. The entry points are live in the app today. The auto-parsing engine is the post-LLP-pilot build.

Survivor portal is live but early

The portal shipped with v1: recovery ring, recovery plan, applications + signature, messages, and document upload. Full public-facing consent infrastructure — for intake links accessible beyond LLP's direct network — is next. The architecture is already there. The guardrails are being finalized.

Six reasons this is
the right platform.

Not better because of a feature list. Better because of what it was built to do — and who built it.

Built for disaster recovery, not adapted for it

Vijilant's data model, triage engine, assessment framework, and workflows are purpose-built for how LLP operates. This is not a nonprofit CRM that disaster workers have learned to use. It's a DCM platform that disaster workers designed.

Triage that computes itself

Every time a case note is saved, a referral outcome is logged, or an assessment is updated, the Auto-Triage Engine re-evaluates the tier. Critical cases don't stay buried. They surface automatically — to the CM, to the supervisor, to the morning brief.

The Recovery Score Ring

No competitor offers an automated recovery visualization. The gradient ring (Red→Coral→Teal→Navy) appears on every client record at two sizes, updating automatically on every assessment save. A supervisor can read an entire caseload at a glance — without opening a single record.

Aid applications that mostly fill themselves

~88% auto-fill from the client profile. The CM reviews what the system couldn't populate, selects the submission method, and clicks submit. The audit trail writes itself. No more rebuilding a 17-field application from scratch for a client whose data already lives in the system.

HIPAA from the ground up

Enterprise BAA. Audit log on every write. Biometric auth with no passcode fallback option. Analytics self-hosted. Zero PHI leaves Vijilant's infrastructure perimeter. Compliance isn't a feature to toggle on — it's the foundation everything else is built on.

The next disaster is the next deployment

Multi-tenancy is in the architecture from day one. After LLP, any DCM organization can configure Vijilant to their caseload, their aid partners, and their intake workflow. RecoverPrepared is a fixed product. Vijilant is a platform — and FEMA-declared disasters happen 65+ times a year.
